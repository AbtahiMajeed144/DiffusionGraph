# Bringing Motion Taxonomies to Continuous Domains via GPLVM on Hyperbolic Manifolds

**Authors:** Noémie Jaquier, Leonel Rozo, Miguel González-Duque, Viacheslav Borovitskiy, Tamim Asfour (2022/2024)

## Theoretical Abstract & Relevance to SEED Project

### Core Theoretical Contribution
This paper introduces a framework for embedding discrete, hierarchical structures (specifically human motion and pose taxonomies) into continuous geometric spaces. The authors argue that Euclidean spaces distort hierarchical tree-like data. To resolve this, they employ a **hyperbolic manifold** (specifically using the Lorentz model), where distances grow exponentially moving away from the origin, naturally accommodating hierarchical structures where shortest paths between nodes tend to pass through common ancestors.

The central model is the **Gaussian Process Hyperbolic Latent Variable Model (GPHLVM)**. The authors construct:
1.  **Hyperbolic Wrapped Gaussian Distributions:** To model uncertainty and density on the hyperbolic manifold.
2.  **Hyperbolic Kernels:** Providing positive-definite approximations for non-Euclidean Gaussian Processes.
3.  **Graph-Distance Inductive Biases:** Unlike fully unsupervised manifold discovery, the model is regularized using a *stress loss* that forces the geodesic distances in the hyperbolic space to match the discrete shortest-path distances of the *supplied* taxonomy graph. Back-constraints are also used to preserve local similarities.
4.  **Geodesic Trajectory Generation:** Once trained, realistic motions between different taxonomy categories (e.g., from one grasp type to another) are generated by following the shortest path (geodesic) in the hyperbolic latent space.

### Relevance to SEED (Semantic Class Graph)
This paper is highly relevant to the SEED project as a **"Novelty Threat / Contrastive Reference"** (categorized under *Novelty threats — read to differentiate* in the project specification). 

1.  **Supplied vs. Discovered Topology:** The most critical distinction between this paper and SEED is that Jaquier et al. *supply* a pre-existing human-designed taxonomy (the graph) and regularize the latent space to fit it. In contrast, SEED proposes to *discover* the generative connectivity graph purely from the geometry of a generative model, investigating whether the emergent topology matches human taxonomy or deviates from it (e.g., perceptual vs. semantic graphs).
2.  **Geodesics as Transitions:** Both projects use geodesics in a continuous space to generate transitions between discrete semantic classes. However, while GPHLVM enforces geodesics to mimic graph edges in a predefined tree, SEED measures where the unsupervised score-Jacobian geodesics naturally route through, deriving the graph structure *a posteriori*.
3.  **Choice of Geometry:** This paper explicitly forces a hyperbolic geometry to accommodate a known hierarchy. SEED operates in the native ambient noise space of a diffusion model and studies what kind of graph structure the learned data manifold naturally induces. 
4.  **Anti-Confounding:** SEED's analysis requires ensuring that the discovered graph is not an artifact. Understanding how explicitly embedded graphs (like in GPHLVM) behave helps in differentiating purely emergent generative connections from forced metric structures.
